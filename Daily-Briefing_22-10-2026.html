📷 Photography — Shoot the brief, not the frame
Commercial clients buy a shot list, not a portfolio
An industrial or tourism client cannot use twenty beautiful frames of the same angle. They need named slots: a wide hero for the website header (16:9, room for overlay text), a tight detail shot, a person-at-work shot with a visible face, a 4:5 crop for Instagram, and one clean frame with deliberate empty space for a headline. Write those five slots down before you leave the house and tick them off on site. Two practical consequences: you stop over-shooting one angle, and your delivery email can say "here are the five assets you asked for" instead of "here are 200 photos" — which is the difference between a one-off and a retainer. For the Highlands in late October, plan around a 15:30 golden hour and bring a cloth; spray off the Firth will be on your front element within ten minutes.

📚 30-Min Learning Focus — Photography
Today: Build a six-slot "Industry & Infrastructure" shot list
Cruise-passenger photography is done until April, so today you pivot the skill toward the sector that is actually hiring on your doorstep: energy and port infrastructure. The exercise is to produce one page you can attach to an email — a defined six-image package for an industrial client — and to understand the exposure problem that kind of site creates (bright sky, dark steel, high contrast).
📖 Free Resource: Cambridge in Colour — Tutorials
cambridgeincolour.com/tutorials.htm — the clearest free written explanations of dynamic range, histograms and exposure bracketing anywhere. Read the "Dynamic Range" and "Using Histograms" pages; they are exactly the problem a steel-and-sky industrial site hands you.
🎬 Free Resource: Sean Tucker (YouTube)
youtube.com/@seantuck — a working commercial photographer who talks about pricing, client briefs and delivering a defined package rather than gear. Search his channel for "clients" and "pricing"; it is the business half of photography, which is the half that gets paid.
⏱ Your 30-min structure:
0–8 min: Read the Cambridge in Colour "Dynamic Range" and "Using Histograms" pages. Write down, in one sentence, when you would bracket instead of trusting a single exposure.
8–16 min: Open the Port of Cromarty Firth and Green Freeport websites and look at the photography they already use. Note what is missing — usually people at work, and clean frames with space for text.
16–26 min: Write your six-slot shot list in a single document: wide hero 16:9, detail, person-at-work, 4:5 social crop, negative-space headline frame, and one dusk/lit exterior. One line each on lens, time of day and why the client needs it.
26–30 min: Save it as "Industry & Infrastructure — Six Image Package" in your portfolio folder. It is now an attachment, not an idea.
