📊 Data Analysis — The one-question rule
One question, one table, one chart
Beginners open a spreadsheet and start poking. Analysts write the question first, in one sentence, before touching the data — "which of my posts got the most saves per 1,000 views?" Then build exactly one table that answers it, and one chart that shows it. If a column does not help answer the question, delete it. This single habit is the difference between three hours of wandering and twenty minutes of work, and it is the thing interviewers actually probe for: not which tool you used, but whether you knew what you were asking.

📚 30-Min Learning Focus — Data Analysis
Today: analyse your own job search
You have 153 briefings behind you and a tracker sitting at zero applications. That gap is today's dataset. Build a two-column sheet — date and jobs found — for the last fourteen days, then add one calculated column: jobs that were a PERFECT or HIGH match. You will finish with a real pivot, a real chart, and the honest number you need to see. This is the same exercise an employer sets at interview, run on data you cannot be vague about.
📖 Free Resource: Kaggle — Pandas micro-course
kaggle.com/learn/pandas — four short lessons, runs in the browser with no install. Do lesson 1 (Creating, Reading and Writing) and lesson 2 (Indexing and Selecting) in one sitting; they are about seven minutes each plus exercises.
🎬 Free Resource: freeCodeCamp — Data Analysis with Python
Search YouTube for "freeCodeCamp Data Analysis with Python" — the full course is free and chaptered. Watch only the Pandas DataFrames chapter today, roughly 25 minutes, and skip the rest.
⏱ Your 30-min structure:
0–05 min — Write your question in one sentence at the top of the sheet: "how many strong matches have I actually seen per week?"
05–15 min — Type the last 14 days into two columns: date, jobs found. Pull the numbers from the archive at the briefings index.
15–25 min — Add a third column for strong matches, then one bar chart of jobs per day. Nothing else. No formatting.
25–30 min — Write two sentences under the chart: what the number is, and what you will change next week. Save it as job-search-analysis.xlsx — it is also an interview talking point.
